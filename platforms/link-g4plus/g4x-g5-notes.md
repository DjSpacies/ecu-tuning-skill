# Link G4X and G5 notes

The adapter in this folder targets Link G4+ (PCLink 5.6.x). This entry records where the later G4X and G5 families
differ, and what a G4X or G5 tune or log needs that the G4+ parser does not give. It is a notes-on-differences entry:
behaviour the G4+ entries already describe is not repeated unless it changed.

Two kinds of point sit here. Bullets tagged `vendor-doc` paraphrase the PCLink G4X/G5 7.8.2 help and the release notes
it carries; they are medium confidence for that version. A firmware number in such a bullet is the earliest G4X / G5
release the help ties to that behaviour; older firmware may not have it, or may treat the setting differently.
Points marked (training), and the derivation text under Why, come from training material (tier B, mostly one
practitioner's live sessions): they stay low confidence until checked against the help for the version in hand. Comparisons with G4+
mean the PCLink 5.6.8 help as summarised in `semantics.md` and `quirks.md`. No G4X/G5 file has been
examined by this project. Where help and training disagree, both are under Disputed / conflicts; the help governs on
what a setting does.

## Claim
G4X and G5 keep the G4+ fuel, ignition and boost concepts (Traditional, Modelled and Modelled Multi-Fuel fuel equations;
MGP or MAP load; dual tables; staged closed-loop boost) but add more drives (G4X up to 8 injector and 8 ignition; G5 up
to 16 and 12), three-stage and (G5 only) direct injection, long-term fuel trim and up to four closed-loop lambda banks,
math-block channels, 4D/5D modifier tables, torque management feeding traction, power management, gear shift and
automatic transmission control, dedicated nitrous and dome boost control, normalised and per-cylinder knock modes, and
reorganised menus (virtual outputs, aux assignment, idle ignition control). Several channels that keep G4+ names sum or
scale differently (Injection Actual PW, Ignition Angle, user CAN values, gear ratio tables), and behaviour depends on the
firmware release as well as the PCLink version. G4X/G5 tunes and logs cannot be read by the G4+ parser, and several
values in them mean something different from what the G4+ adapter assumes.

## Why (derivation)

### What changed from G4+ (training material)
- **Speed.** A side-by-side comparison showed injector pulse width reacting visibly sooner to throttle blips on G4X, credited to a faster processor; the practitioner links this to better transient fuel and spark and tighter closed-loop control (lambda, idle, boost, cam). Onboard logging is quoted at about 100 Hz on G4+ against about 1000 Hz on G4X, with more memory and a much faster download (the help's per-channel ceiling is under Logging in `g4x-g5-io.md`; see Disputed there).
- **Math blocks.** G4X offers up to eight user equations built from existing channels, which G4+ lacks. They can be logged, displayed, or used as an axis for fuel, spark or boost tables.
- **Cam control.** The practitioner finds G4X cam control quicker to set up than G4+: manufacturer presets (for example a Honda K-series mode) bring tested PID gains, and a cam angle test in calibrate mode learns tooth offsets. A cold-oil compensation is mentioned for phaser response during warm-up (the help's version is under Cam control in `g4x-g5-ignition.md`).
- **Idle ignition.** The G4+ 3D table of idle error against ignition change is replaced on G4X by a simpler PID-style idle ignition control (the help calls it PD; see Disputed in `g4x-g5-ignition.md`).
- **Virtual outputs.** On G4X the virtual aux page only offers off, test or on; the switching conditions are set on a general-purpose output whose function is set to drive that virtual aux. A G4+ user looking for a condition list on the virtual aux page will not find one.
- **Aux assignment.** The aux page shows a locked function; the assignment is made from the function's own setup window (for example the engine fan function chooses its aux output).
- **Closed-loop lambda update rate** tops out at 10 on G4X; the practitioner says G4+ allowed higher numbers, so a G4+ value does not transfer directly.
- **Knock.** A normalised mode judges spikes against the sensor's overall output pattern instead of a fixed threshold table; response can be spark, fuel or both. Per-cylinder gain and trim tables are available on boxes with a knock board (entry-level boxes lack one; the help scopes this by hardware version, see Disputed in `g4x-g5-ignition.md`).
- **Traction.** Besides slip-based traction control there is a power management feature (race-timer target speed or open-loop reduction tables), and an onboard accelerometer can provide longitudinal g as an axis where no undriven wheel exists (not on every box; see Disputed in `g4x-g5-fuel.md`).
- **File interchange.** A G4X calibration file has one structure across all box types, plug-ins included, so a same-engine file from another box can seed a new base map with its trigger, cam and fuel setup; only the I/O pins need reassigning.
### How the fuel equations relate (shared with G4+, seen on G4X/G5)
- **Traditional** is an injection-time table: cell value times a master pulse width, then compensations. One course calls it a hybrid whose cells behave much like an unscaled VE table. With load set to MAP the ECU also scales fuel by MAP in the background, and with open-loop lambda correction enabled it scales fuel by the lambda target table, so the target table and the fuel table interact as in a VE model. A tuner who leaves the target table at placeholder values before tuning in this configuration bakes those placeholders into the fuel table.
- **Modelled** estimates air mass from displacement, cylinder count, VE, MAP (or the chosen equation load) and charge temperature, then fuel mass = air mass / (target lambda × stoich AFR), then pulse width from injector flow, fuel density, differential pressure, dead time and short-pulse adder. The master fuel number disappears because injector scaling lives in the injector setup.
- **Modelled Multi-Fuel** interpolates stoich, density and density temperature coefficient between the primary fuel (main fuel page) and the secondary fuel (multi-fuel page) by sensed ethanol content through a blend ratio table (the help blends more properties; see Multi fuel and Disputed in `g4x-g5-fuel.md`). The VE table is shared, so a Modelled petrol tune carries straight over; moving from Traditional to either Modelled mode means retuning the whole fuel table because the numbers mean different things.
- **Charge cooling coefficient** (default 10 in the sources) is a Link model term for the cooling effect of evaporating fuel on the charge. If it is wrong the model delivers the right lambda at the target it was tuned at but misses when the target is changed.
- **Charge temperature approximation** blends IAT and ECT by an airflow-indexed table: at low airflow the charge picks up heat from the ports and runs closer to ECT; at high airflow it approaches IAT. The right blend depends on where the IAT sensor sits.
## Assumptions
- `vendor-doc` points describe the PCLink G4X/G5 7.8.2 help. Firmware numbers quoted with them come from release notes in that help; a tune from an older release may lack a feature or default it differently.
- Training material reflects the PCLink G4X/G5 versions current when it was recorded; no version numbers are given in the records, and menu names may have moved since.
- Most G4X training points come from one practitioner's live builds on specific cars (K-series, SR20DET, RB25DET, supercharged and drag S2000). G5 training points come from one course build (a 4G63) and one technical session on the charge cooling coefficient.
- The training fuel-model descriptions assume port injection on gasoline, ethanol blends or methanol. Direct injection appears only in the help, and only on DI-capable G5 hardware.

## Breaks when
- **Box tier.** A file moved to a lower-tier box (fewer I/O, no internal wideband controller, no knock board) silently drops the assignments that box cannot hold. Small boxes such as the Atom have only four aux outputs. (training) Lower-tier injector drivers are saturated types rated around 2 A, so low-impedance injectors need ballast resistors, and paralleling several injectors on one driver (as done on an Extreme driving twelve injectors from eight drivers) needs a current check first. The help puts all current G4X/G5 drivers in the saturated group (see Injector drives and Disputed in `g4x-g5-fuel.md`).
- **Version mismatch.** Firmware older than the PC software can make outputs deviate from what the software commands. Updating takes a minute or two and must not be interrupted; a weak battery or a key cycle mid-update can corrupt the unit. `vendor-doc`: PCLink and firmware must match, and a saved tune restores exactly only onto the firmware it came from.
- **Firmware crossing.** `vendor-doc`: several releases moved trigger offsets, changed sensor calibrations or rescaled settings (see Firmware-gated changes); a tune carried across one can run with wrong base timing, load or CAN values without any error.
- **Unstored changes.** After a file is sent to the ECU, declining the store prompt means a key cycle reverts to the previous calibration. Some mode changes only populate their tables after store and a power cycle (an idle target table that did not appear until then is one example).
- **Base-file drift.** One anecdote: after a custom base file was uploaded the stoich setting read 14.1 instead of the 14.7 entered. Treat as a single observation, but it is why the sources audit key settings after every upload.
- **Plug-in base maps** start a stock donor car only: they assume stock injectors and no forced induction, have few strategies enabled, and on some chassis drive the dash through the serial port mode (a generic file can leave the cluster dead). One base file shipped with launch control active; another (a Honda DC5 map) had a single fuel table, and enabling dual tables gave table 2 a single scalar with no axes. The Link K-series base file had no VTEC off delay. `vendor-doc`: the help itself says sample maps set the I/O for the model but their fuel and ignition numbers are not tuning recommendations.
- **Analog lambda.** A mis-scaled 0 to 5 V wideband corrupts display, closed loop and lean protection together; a CAN wideband avoids the scaling step. With a narrowband, Traditional-mode closed loop chases a voltage and ignores the target table.

### Handling a G4X or G5 tune or log in this skill
- The parser reads only G4+ `.pclr` tunes and `.llg` logs. `vendor-doc` file types: a G4X calibration is `.pclx` and a G4X log `.llgx`; a G5 calibration is `.pcl5` and a G5 log `.llg5`. A calibration holds tables, settings, CAN setup, statistics and fault codes; a log holds channel data alone. Refuse either kind of file, ask for a CSV export or screenshots, and always ask for the calibration behind a log, since the log cannot show what produced it. Never map these tables through `tables.yaml`. The G4+ quirks (`quirks-io.md`) already note that one name can map to several DataIds across generations.
- Log rate can be about ten times that of G4+ (training; the help's limits are under Logging in `g4x-g5-io.md`). Resample before comparing with G4+ logs, and expect large files.
- Math-block channels are user-named and can be table axes. Do not infer what a math channel holds from its name; ask, or look for the defining equation. Common uses in the training are uncorrected lambda (measured lambda with the closed-loop trim removed), target-minus-measured lambda or AFR for lean protection, and MAP divided by exhaust manifold pressure as a fuel load term.
- `vendor-doc` Math blocks work internally in metric units (kPa, lambda, deg C, km/h, litres, l/100 km, m) whatever the display units, so a threshold that looks like psi or AFR is really kPa or lambda. There are eight blocks; each takes up to four runtimes and a 30-character equation, and all are evaluated at 250 Hz. The special functions, each usable once per block, are peak hold (timeout 0 holds indefinitely), windowed change, an average of 20 samples spread over a window, seconds elapsed, a 1 ms counter and a rising-edge counter. Equality is now an exact float compare; older firmware converted to integer first, so `=` logic may behave differently after an update.
- Expect more tables per role: dual fuel and ignition tables switched by a virtual aux or the VTEC output, two cam target tables switched by VTEC, two boost target tables and two spark tables interpolated by ethanol content, 4D/5D fuel and ignition modifiers gated by a switch, gear or timer, and general-purpose boost sub-tables. A single "fuel table" is often not the whole story.
- `vendor-doc` Disabled is not the same as Off (for cylinder trims, launch, torque request and similar functions). Disabled stops the function but leaves its tables allocated and on screen; Off deletes them. Tables present in a tune therefore prove nothing about whether the function runs.
- Fuel table cells mean VE (percent cylinder filling) only in Modelled modes. In Traditional mode they are percentages of master pulse width. Read the fuel equation mode before interpreting any fuel number.
- Load axes: fuel tables in the training usually sit on MGP (MAP minus baro); Link base maps put ignition on absolute MAP. Alpha-N setups put fuel on TPS and may set the equation load source to baro. Check the axis channel of each table.
- `vendor-doc` G4+ table import works, but fixed-axis tables may lose breakpoints. Inspect the axes afterwards; if they are wrong, type them in and paste the cells only.
- `vendor-doc` Undo: Ctrl+Z reverses single manual edits (even stored ones) but never Quick Tune or Mixture Map writes. To reverse those, use Undo Short Term Changes, power-cycle before storing, or reload a saved map.
- `vendor-doc` Units: MAP Delta is in kPa per 100 ms and TPS Delta in % per 100 ms; throttle differential pressure is pre-throttle pressure minus MAP. Throttle Safety Active means the engine was stopped by a throttle sensor error.
- `vendor-doc` Error signs differ per channel, so check the sign before calling a value over or under. Lambda Target Error = target − Lambda Avg (positive means richer than asked). Boost Target Error = target − measured (positive means short of target). Power management speed error = target − actual (negative means ahead of target). Gear shift RPM Target Error = engine rpm − shift target. For Idle Target Error the help contradicts itself (see Disputed in `g4x-g5-control.md`).
- `vendor-doc` A G5 on USB power only (main power off) shows 0 on its voltage runtimes and keeps its outputs off. This is not a sensor fault.
- Wheel speed names are a trap. In this software "driving wheel speed" means the powered wheels and the other source is ground speed, but the practitioner's own wording swaps "driven" and "driving" between sessions (training). `vendor-doc` confirms that Driving means the wheels connected to the drivetrain and Driven the unpowered ones; in wheelspin Driving exceeds Driven; distance and economy use Driven; and % Slip needs both sources. % Slip = (Driving − Driven) / Driven × 100, so it is 0 % when they are equal, 100 % when Driving is double, negative under braking, and −100 % if the driving wheels lock while the driven ones still turn. G4+ text uses "driven" loosely (see Disputed in `g4x-g5-io.md`). Confirm which physical sensor feeds each channel before computing slip.
### Setup checks the sources run on these boxes
- **MAP calibration interlock.** (training) No injector or coil output fires until a key-on MAP-to-baro calibration is done. Run it, plus TPS calibration, after every new upload, alpha-N engines included. Close agreement (about 100.1 against 100.2 kPa in one session) confirms scaling and wiring. `vendor-doc`: the offset step is refused when baro and engine-off MAP are more than 5 kPa apart. A larger gap points at a wrong calibration or a damaged sensor (commonly water in a poorly mounted unit, or wiring); fix that instead of forcing an offset.
- `vendor-doc` Internal MAP on G4X plug-in boards: older boards are 4.0 bar absolute (about 3 bar boost) and newer ones 7.0 bar absolute (about 6 bar boost), with a 7 bar sticker; Monsoon units are 7 bar from serial 106244. Pick whichever calibration reads baro with the hose off. Among wire-ins only the G4X Monsoon has an internal sensor; the Atom, Storm, Xtreme, Fury and Voodoo Pro need an external one. (One plug-in page disagrees on the range; see Disputed in `g4x-g5-control.md`.)
- `vendor-doc` MAP filter: use the smallest value that removes genuine noise. Heavy filtering makes load lag, increases accel fuel demand and can make the engine feel flat. The filter has been configurable since PCLink 6.16.26.
- `vendor-doc` The Link 3 bar MAP calibration is linear, from about 0.3 V at 20 kPa up to 4.9 V at 300 kPa absolute. Other named calibrations cover 1.15, 2.5, 4, 5 and 6.5 bar. (Release 6.24.370 changed one point; see Disputed in `g4x-g5-io.md`.)
- **Input pull-ups.** (training) A Hall cam sensor (K-series) shows a cam signal error until the digital input pull-up is enabled. NTC inputs normally use the internal 1 k pull-up; turn it off when the signal is shared with a factory ECU.
- `vendor-doc` Digital input thresholds are 1.8 V rising and 1.0 V falling; any DI can read frequency or duty beyond 10 kHz; VVT cam signals go only on DI 1-4. G4+ differed (low below 1 V, high above 2 V, frequency on DI 1-6 only, up to 500 Hz). Spare analog inputs can act as DIs. A G5 ADIO pin can be a switch or analog input, or a switch or PWM output, but never a frequency or duty input, so flex fuel and wheel speed sensors need real DI pins.
- `vendor-doc` The DI latch option turns a momentary button into a toggle. A pin's connection list names only its direct users, not uses as a table axis or a GP output condition, so a pin can appear free while it is in use.
- `vendor-doc` An Temp pull-ups: the Storm and Monsoon have a fixed 1 kohm; the remaining G4X models and the Voodoo Pro have An Temp 1-2 selectable between 1 k, 10 k and off, with 3-4 fixed at 1 k; the remaining G5 models have all four selectable. (G4+ offers 1 to 47 kohm on An Temp 1-2.) A fixed pull-up always loads the line, so it can be unsuitable for sensors with a voltage output. With the pull-up off, type in the external resistor value for ohm calibrations.
- `vendor-doc` Calibrations: there are three linear two-point calibrations, which extrapolate beyond their points, and ten tables, which interpolate, clamp to the end cells and may be in ohms so that the pull-up is allowed for. Past its range a linear calibration can give impossible numbers, while a table calibration goes flat. This replaces the G4+ split of custom tables 1-3 / 4-6 / 7-10. For a frequency-output MAF on a custom table, an input unit of ohms gives a wide enough axis.
- `vendor-doc` Faults: user-threshold codes exist for high MAP (overboost or a dead sensor) and high ECT. CAN lambda errors come from the device's own error report rather than from voltage. Analog status shows Inactive only for pins set as DIs. For TPS, code 67 is the general main fault and 78 is the same fault with e-throttle, which shuts the throttle (the plate returns to its sprung rest and the error value is used for fuel and spark).
- `vendor-doc` While the lock timing switch is active, ignition is held at the trigger calibration reference.
- `vendor-doc` EGT: no G4X or G5 has a direct thermocouple input; use an external amplifier into an analog pin, or a CAN thermocouple module.
- `vendor-doc` Accelerometer (G5, and G4X except the Atom, Monsoon and Storm): zero it while level at ride height (vertical then reads 1 g) and set the heading correction so that forward acceleration appears as longitudinal (90 deg swaps longitudinal with lateral).
- `vendor-doc` G5 internal GPS: inertial fusion gives updates at up to 50 Hz, and dead reckoning works after orientation learning. To learn it, stay stationary outside until a 3D fix; then drive roughly ten cumulative left and right turns of 90 deg or more each, plus 100 m or more in a straight line at 40 km/h or faster, until the status shows dead reckoning. The time zone offset takes no account of daylight saving.
- **Knock disable input.** (training) If the knock-disable digital input is set to always on, knock control is bypassed silently. `vendor-doc` (PCLink 6.17.22 on): the defaults are disable input off, max retard 0 and max enrichment 0, so newly enabled knock control detects and logs knock but corrects nothing until limits are entered. Verify these settings in any tune said to have knock protection.
- `vendor-doc` Knock hardware: the G4X Atom and Monsoon below hardware V3.0 have none (the knock overview reads as if no Atom has it, while the Atom pinout gives knock to V3+ units; see Disputed in `g4x-g5-ignition.md`). On a Monsoon V3+ the knock pin is shared with DI 4, so it can serve only one of the two.
- `vendor-doc` Changing trigger mode reloads that mode's presets, trigger offset included (as on G4+). On a rotary with separate leading and trailing plugs, light both sparks while setting the offset. After an update, recheck base timing (see Firmware-gated changes).
- **Boost cut is relative to the boost target.** (training) On a car running wastegate spring only, the practitioner still enables closed-loop boost, with all duties and the minimum clamp at zero and a target near spring pressure, so that the overboost cut has a target to compare against. The help describes fixed-value MAP limits only (see Disputed in `g4x-g5-control.md`).
- **Staged closed-loop boost.** (training) Control steps from a fixed pre-spool duty (stage 1) to a base-duty stage (stage 2) to PID (stage 3) as MAP closes on target; the thresholds are per-tune settings (40 kPa in one tune; 100 and 15 kPa in another). If only stage 2 is allowed, duty latches at its last value when stage 3 would take over, instead of following the base duty table; the practitioner's workaround is to allow stages 2 and 3 with the PID gains at zero. Closed loop cannot run across two solenoids at once; with top and bottom port solenoids he runs the top one from a feed-forward table and the second one open loop. The help's stages differ (see Boost control and Disputed in `g4x-g5-control.md`).
- **Closed-loop startup lockout** (training) defaults to about 5 s; an external wideband may need about 30 s of heater warm-up, so the practitioner lengthens it.
- **Injector test.** (training) The basic test fires repeatedly until stopped, by default at 2 ms and 10 Hz. The pump is off, yet residual rail pressure can still flood a cylinder, so pull the injector from the rail or use the advanced fixed-count test. `vendor-doc` (7.8.2): the chosen drive fires a set count at a set frequency and width, then stops by itself (30 pulses at 10 Hz and 5 ms take 3 s); see Disputed in `g4x-g5-fuel.md`. With drive reallocation the selector is a fuel channel, so the test also verifies the channel-to-drive mapping. For new injector wiring, have the fuel system unprimed (to avoid flooding) but not bone dry (to avoid wear), and confirm that only the intended injector clicks.
- `vendor-doc` First start in Modelled mode: enter the real engine capacity, the fuel properties, the base fuel pressure, and the injector flow with its rated pressure. Then drop injector flow a little for a temporarily rich start, and restore the real figure before tuning, or the VE table soaks up the error. In Traditional mode Master Fuel acts as a global trim, and higher is richer.
- `vendor-doc` Injector flow is entered per cylinder. With one injector per cylinder, enter its flow; on a V8 firing four injectors, enter half of one injector's flow; with staged injection, enter the primary flow per cylinder.
- `vendor-doc` With no fuel pressure or exhaust pressure sensor fitted, the input should be off or read zero. A non-zero exhaust pressure channel distorts internal and CAN-Lambda results.
- `vendor-doc` After changing the injection mode, store, cycle the ignition, then judge the result (as in the help's MX5 GTX steps). This backs the store-and-cycle point under Breaks when.
- `vendor-doc` Firmware update sequence: save the tune, store, power-cycle, and set the connection speed to fast. Afterwards review all new settings, which arrive at their defaults.
- `vendor-doc` An encrypted save locks a tune to one ECU serial, but it conceals the settings only when the ECU is also password protected; without that, the file remains readable and editable.
- `vendor-doc` If the Ignition Switch function is assigned and its input is inactive, fuel and ignition are 100 % cut; an active Stop Switch does the same. With no switch wired, set the function to Off or Always On. Engine Kill may also come from ECU hold power, anti-theft or over-voltage.
- `vendor-doc` Stepper idle valves home after key-off and so need ECU hold power. On G5 the internal power control keeps that wiring simple; G4X must use the external relay schemes, wired exactly. Homing at key-on instead can flare the start or lengthen cranking. A unipolar (6-wire) stepper supplied separately from the ECU can backfeed through the aux outputs and keep the ECU alive after key-off; power both from one source, such as the hold-power relay.
- `vendor-doc` Idle actuator wiring. A 4-wire stepper has its coils on fixed output pairs (e.g. aux 5/6, 7/8); find the coils with an ohmmeter, and reverse direction with the stepper type or by swapping a single pair, never both. A 3-wire solenoid reads more than 10 ohm from common to coil on each side; the closing coil goes on the pair's first output, and a reversed install is fixed by inverting that output's active state. A 2-wire solenoid goes on aux 1-10, or on ignition drives 1-8 only with an external flywheel diode; its polarity does not matter. The Atom, Monsoon, Voodoo Neo 4 and Neo 6 cannot drive a 4-wire stepper, and the Atom and Monsoon cannot drive a 6-wire one.
- **Communication.** (training) Leave the connection speed on fast and drop to slow if the link drops out; set port and mode to auto. Page layouts are not saved automatically, and the software keeps only the latest save, so save staged copies of the calibration. `vendor-doc` (PCLink 6.19.65 on): USB connection mode, which Auto also uses, is the recommended port choice.
- `vendor-doc` G5 WiFi works only as an access point on most units (client mode exists only on the Voodoo Pro) and can run alongside USB as a second connection. Firmware cannot be loaded over WiFi, the WiFi settings are not in the basemap, and changes to them need a store plus a power cycle.
- **CAN.** (training) G4X boxes terminate the bus internally at their end; a far-end 120 ohm terminator is still needed once several devices share the bus (one CAN wideband tolerated its absence, an AEM X-Series did not). All devices on one bus share a bit rate; transmit rates can differ. The Link keypad defaults to 1 Mbit/s and CAN ID 405, some other keypads to 500 kbit/s.
### Tuning aids
- `vendor-doc` Quick Trim takes target and measured lambda from the log cursor, plus the closed-loop correction (only when CLL is on in single-bank mode; otherwise 0 %), so active trim is included. That field can be edited between −50 and 50 % (e.g. to enter long-term trim). This differs from the G4+ quirk in which Mixture Map ignores closed-loop trim (`quirks-fuel.md`). Quick Trim works one cell at a time unless a block has similar values.
- `vendor-doc` Quick Tune writes a cell only if lambda is outside the precision band (0.05, for example, gives 0.95-1.05 on a 1.00 target), rpm and load are inside the centre tolerances, the stabilise time has elapsed, and no lockout is active; it usually takes 1 to 3 attempts per cell. Filter 1 means undamped. The lockouts are rpm over idle (about 1000 rpm), load over the overrun row (about −80 kPa MGP or 20 kPa MAP; −100 kPa MGP if full vacuum is wanted), and ECT warm enough for zero warm-up enrichment.
- `vendor-doc` Tune-ahead lifts the copied value by its trim at each step (50 at 5 % gives 52.5, 55, 57.5). Right-and-down fill rewrites a cell only when the new value beats the last stored one, so returning to lower cells does not overwrite them.
### Other functions
- `vendor-doc` Subaru TGV (from G4X 6.16.27): both motors are wired in series on a push-pull pair, with two feedbacks (about 4.2 V shut, 0.4 V open). The valves open above 2 % throttle or 65 C ECT, so they are shut only at cold idle, where the mixture runs very rich; correct this with a fuel overlay on TGV position. A TPS or APS is required.
- `vendor-doc` Canister purge has no tuning settings. It opens at light load only (MAP below 90 kPa, above 1800 rpm, plus one further condition), so purge-related lambda error shows off idle rather than at idle.
- `vendor-doc` Intercooler spray uses an enable input plus a chosen runtime: it sprays above ON and stops below OFF. A typical period is 5 to 10 s, duty 25 to 50 %, and rpm lockout 400 (0 for bench testing). Resume carries on the cycle, so spraying can begin in the off phase; Reset sprays straight away.
- `vendor-doc` GP PWM runs a 3D duty table at a fixed or table-driven frequency while its on conditions hold, with an off delay; uses include progressive nitrous and water injection.
- `vendor-doc` Turbo speed: shaft speed is calculated from pulse frequency using the compressor blade count and the sensor divider.
### Firmware-gated changes
- `vendor-doc` Fuel and lambda features by first release. G4X 6.16.15: accel sensitivity table, negative warm-up, ethanol duty clamp, Modelled Multi Fuel and selectable CLL lambda sources (when updating from older firmware, set up the accel table and CLL sources by hand, because they do not carry over). 6.18.8: LTT. 6.19.37: wall wetting. 6.21.12: CLL reactivation in samples. 6.22.8: lambda overlay gating and pump loop rates. 6.24.370 / 7.1.370: drive reallocation and fuel-mass accel. 6.26.11 / 7.3.11: three-stage injection, negative dead time and four-bank CLL. 6.30.2 / 7.7.2: test modes off at power-up. Older tunes may lack these or treat them differently.
- `vendor-doc` Releases that moved timing. Mitsubishi Colt mode now needs a new offset, with Trigger 2 rising and Trigger 1 falling. The EVO 10 offset was 360 deg wrong between 6.26.11/7.3.11 and the 6.29.5/7.6.5 fix. Subaru V7-10 moved 360 deg in 6.16.27. The sync-on-first-gap fix (6.20.15) and the reluctor edge fix (6.15.18) both require the offset to be re-verified. After any update, confirm 0 deg at TDC 1 with a timing light before applying load, especially if the release notes name your trigger mode.
- `vendor-doc` In G4X 6.17.8 the first arming threshold cell changed from 0 to 500 rpm; check that cell on reluctor setups after updating.
- `vendor-doc` In PCLink 6.19.105, 7.1.21 and 7.6.5 the vendor altered sample map offsets (ST185/ST205 by 360 deg; Nissan 360 optical maps), so do not trust sample-map offsets across PCLink versions. The GPS time zone offset needs re-entering after a tune moves between firmware versions.
- `vendor-doc` In G4X 6.24.370 / G5 7.1.370 the 4.75 V point of the Link 3 bar MAP calibration moved from 290 to 300 kPa. Tunes made on the earlier version read about 10 kPa low near full scale, so review load-indexed tables after updating.
- `vendor-doc` GP RPM calibration was in rev/s before G4X 6.21.12, so older GP RPM calibrations are off by a factor of 60.
- `vendor-doc` In G4X 6.18.8 the MoTeC E888 CAN analog multiplier changed 1000-fold, so recalibrate E888 inputs; GP pressure resolution became 0.2 kPa (from 0.1), with a 13000 kPa ceiling; and the upgrade wiped the ECU statistics.
- `vendor-doc` G4X 6.19.37 added tacho offset and max rpm, corrected the rotary and two-stroke factor, changed the speedo output from frequency to speed units, merged staged accel fuel into one setting, and added normalised knock modes. Afterwards recheck tacho max rpm and multiplier (rotary or two-stroke), speedo max and offset, and staged accel fuel.
- `vendor-doc` PCLink 6.22.25 and 6.16.26 raised the MAP limit setting maximum from 500 to 1000 kPa, changed single launch rpm resolution from 100 rpm to 1 rpm, and added a configurable MAP filter and an exhaust pressure input.
### Plug-in specific notes
- `vendor-doc` Sample maps get the model's I/O right and nothing more. Every plug-in should have at least MAP and IAT sensors fitted, even if the factory system lacked them (early Evo I-VIII have no IAT usable by the charge temperature model).
- **Injectors and fuel.** `vendor-doc` These have no direct low-impedance drive (high-impedance injectors or ballast only): G5 Nissan Y61 Gen2 TB48; the Mazda and Mitsubishi plug-ins; Subaru V1-11; MR2Link V1 and V2-3; AltezzaLink; BMWLink; GTTLink; GTRLink; 300ZLink; S13Link; S15Link; 350ZLink.
- `vendor-doc` High-numbered injector drives are often unavailable or tied to other hardware (main relay hold, ISC, a diode to 12 V, high-side conversion): Inj 8 on TTLink, EVO IV-VIII and JZX90; Inj 7-8 on BMWLink; Inj 7 on GTRLink and S15Link. Read the pinout before using one for staging. On WRXLink (11), injectors 5-8 sit on an expansion header and 8 is not brought out, so secondaries can only be group-fired (contradicted elsewhere on that page; see Disputed in `g4x-g5-fuel.md`). The DC5 Integra Type R has drives 5-8 at its main header, so sequential staging then needs only harness work.
- `vendor-doc` GTRLink in an RB20DET loom has injectors 4 and 6 crossed, which puts cylinder trims and sequential timing on the wrong cylinders (one lean, one rich); swap those pins. R34 GT-R boards before V1.7 also need the A/C relay fed from ignition-switched power to stop battery drain.
- `vendor-doc` On a WRX RA with a factory fifth injector, the plug-in does not drive that injector and it stays open; disconnect it or remove the wire. With the WRXLink 04/07 base map in a car that lacks a fuel temperature sensor, the open-circuit reading skews fuel through the fuel temperature trim; disable that input.
- `vendor-doc` GT86/BRZ: jumpers on injector outputs 1-4 either condition the signals for the factory DI driver box or pass them straight through; pass-through suits direct port injectors only (swaps). The factory DI box (DI injectors, HP pump) is powered only while a designated aux is held active low, so check that first when DI is dead. The plug-in itself inverts the injector signal and adds the pull-up, so leave injector drive mode at Standard (unlike a wire-in Voodoo Pro with an FA20). The ECU reads only the factory narrowband, not the wide-range sensor, so add an aftermarket wideband. On key-start cars cranking needs the starter output pulled low; push-button cars use it to run the starter. Facelift LIN alternators sit near 13.8 V without ECU control. Storing may light the ABS and CE lamps; a key cycle clears them.
- `vendor-doc` JZX90 conversion to sequential: run drives 4-6 from the expansion plug, one per cylinder (cylinder 1 at the front), select sequential, and verify with the injector test. MX5Link with a Mazda GTX: set Multi-Point Group on INJ1-2, store, and cycle the ignition.
- `vendor-doc` An NA 1.6 MX5 without its vane AFM starts and then stalls once cranking ends, because its pump relay used to be held via an AFM contact; trigger the relay from a spare aux configured as Fuel Pump. On a DC5 lacking the factory immobiliser, the pump may be on a different header pin (on boards V1.7 and older move the wire; from V1.8 use a jumper). The S2000 AP1 fuel pump pin has a USDM/JDM market jumper; with the wrong setting the pump never runs.
- **Ignition and triggers.** `vendor-doc` Internal igniters: the RZR XP900 has them on Ign 1-2 only (Ign 3 upward are logic level and need smart coils or an igniter); BMWLink has them (moving to an external igniter or smart coils alters every ignition output); so does R53 MiniLink.
- `vendor-doc` CivicLink 95/98 with the factory Honda igniter: set spark edge to Rising, store, power down, then reconnect the coil. The header has four ignition outputs (Ign 1 on the distributor pin), for the stock distributor or multi-coil.
- `vendor-doc` S2000 AP1: Ign 5-6 carry VTEC and tacho hardware and cannot be used for coils; injector drives 7-8 exist only from bottom board 1.6; older boards duplicate some header pins on the expansion connector (DI 1, e-throttle relay Aux 6), and the duplicate must stay unconnected.
- `vendor-doc` MX5Link jumper: on a 1.8 L NA (coil packs with igniters inside) fit it across both pins; on a 1.6 L (separate igniter) remove it. Direct spark upgrades exist for the MX5 NA with Ign 3-4 at the main header, the MX5 NB via its expansion connector, MiniLink PCB V1.1 onward, the JZX100, MR2Link V1, and the newest VLLink with Ign 1-6 on expansion connector two; then set ignition mode to Direct Spark and store.
- `vendor-doc` GT86/BRZ 2017+ on bottom boards before revision 05: the facelift brings a Hall-type crank sensor and an inverted DI fuel pressure sensor with 4 pins. Set Trigger 1 to optical/Hall with the pull-up on; pull the new pressure input up to 5 V through 8.2 kOhm; move the DI pressure source there; and calibrate 4.06 V = 0 kPa, 0.46 V = 30000 kPa. Revision 05+ boards need only the right sample map. Base map selection depends on key or push start, pre-2017 or facelift, and bottom board ≤ 04 or ≥ 05. Auto and manual cars share maps.
- `vendor-doc` JZX90 (bottom board V1.5 or older): hard starting or no start when hot, with cranking rpm at 0 or erratic even though the scope looks fine, means the traction ECU tapping the crank signal is adding DC offset; disconnect the tap. On V1.4 and older, swap two idle stepper wires if idle misbehaves. Factory TRC is not supported.
- `vendor-doc` MX5 NB2 bottom board revision 04 or older has a VVT hardware fault; check the board before VVT PID work.
- **Sensors and inputs.** `vendor-doc` Factory MAP ranges limit boost: the Civic 95 reads to about 1.2 bar boost; the GT86/BRZ sensor is NA-range with no boost reading; the RB25DET (GTTLink) and RX7 are as in the G4+ notes (`quirks-io.md`). Evo IV-VIII sensors vary by year. 2011-era WRX/STI use the Subaru V9 (most STI) or V7-8 (lesser WRX) calibration: try one, check that MAP equals baro with the engine off, and otherwise use the other. The S2000 takes the Honda 92-99 calibration. On the supercharged Mini the sensor is downstream of the supercharger and needs the BMW Mini calibration.
- `vendor-doc` Board jumpers change what an input reads. The Evo IX, DC5 and JZX90 choose factory or internal MAP on one input, and the software calibration must agree (the DC5 sample map expects the factory sensor). The MX5 NB1/NB2 has MAF pull-down jumpers; remove them if the input carries anything else. S15 J8 is a pull-up that enables an IAT on the AFM wire. GT86 An Temp 4 is either an expansion pin or the battery temperature sensor. WRX11 moved its little-used analog inputs to the expansion connector. The JZX100 can put 5 V onto the MAF plug for a MAP sensor. Nonsense readings on a plug-in may come from a jumper, not the sensor.
- `vendor-doc` Speed-density conversions that reuse AFM wiring for MAP or IAT (Evo IX, early WRX, MX5 NB): MAP needs a real 5 V supply (the AFM feed is 12 V); take out the AFM pull-down; an NTC on a voltage input needs a 1 kohm pull-up; and make the old AFM plug impossible to connect, or airflow voltage lands on an input calibrated as MAP. Evo IV-VIII An Volt 5 has a fixed 4.7 kohm pull-up to 5 V, so treat it as a resistance input.
- `vendor-doc` A factory IAT inside the AFM or airbox, or used as an ambient sensor (FZJ100Link, GT86/BRZ in the MAF, MX5Link, WRXLink 7-9), is pre-compressor and slow: adequate on an NA engine, not on a boosted one. Add a fast IAT after the intercooler and ahead of the throttle, on the old wiring or a free input. STI sensors (WRXLink 04/07/11) sit by the throttle body and so already give charge temperature.
- `vendor-doc` G5 Y61 TB48: the only internal MAP option is 4 bar absolute (about 3 bar boost), and the sample tune takes load from it, so connect a manifold hose to the ECU's port before the first start; alternatively MAP can come in on the spare MAF wire. A second H-bridge is available for a second e-throttle.
- `vendor-doc` The NA6 manual MX5 has only idle and full-throttle switches; add an analog TPS before relying on accel enrichment or throttle-axis tables. The Mazdaspeed MX5 (NB2) has a second factory IAT on another input. The JZX100 5-wire MAF has an IAT pair (early cars pins 1-2, later 4-5) that reads about 2.21-2.69 kohm at 20 C.
- **Configuration, CAN and e-throttle.** `vendor-doc` WRXLink (11) checks after loading the sample map: CAN mode (step through the modes with power cycles until the ABS/DCCD lamps go out; the mode name need not match the car); capacity EJ20 or EJ25 (a wrong value distorts the Modelled equation); on intake-only AVCS, disabling the exhaust cam input and output (sample maps expect four cams and otherwise raise VVT errors); and the USDM O2 heater output. To tell WRX11 from WRX11USDM, look at cavities 1-3 of plug B134: wires there mean USDM. There is no FA20 support, and 350ZLink lacks VQ35HR support.
- `vendor-doc` DC5: the alternator line selects between two charge voltages (pulled low about 13 V, released about 14.5 V) through a GP output; Aux 15 must stay on for the ECU to operate; the A/C request arrives over K-line as a CAN DI. GTTLink tacho duty is 20 % for RB20DE/RB25DE and 70 % for RB25DET.
- `vendor-doc` With GTTLink fitted to a 4wd R34 non-GTR, a 4wd Stagea, or a car with factory traction control, the engine runs but 4wd and TCS lose their data (warning lamps, possibly wrong 4wd behaviour); those cars need the GTR-type plug-in.
- `vendor-doc` Plug-in e-throttle: BMWLink exposes no H-bridges, so two plain auxes command an external controller, along with the redundant sensors. S15Link (bottom board V1.8 on) and GTRLink (V1.7 on) bring out a throttle relay, analog inputs and H-bridges, while earlier boards rely on an external controller. MiniLink and EVOLink IV-VIII assign the required e-throttle relay to an unconnected aux, which therefore does nothing.

## Worked example

### Idle airflow check (Modelled mode)
At warm idle the practitioner read estimated air per cylinder of 0.12 g at about 920 rpm on a 2.4 L four-cylinder.
Air mass flow = rpm / 120 × cylinders × g/cyl = 920 / 120 × 4 × 0.12 = 3.7 g/s (3.7 × 10⁻³ kg/s).
His guide for a warm four-cylinder of that size is about 4 to 5 g/s, so the model is under-reading air: VE at idle is low, not the injector data. He multiplied idle VE by about 1.05 (to about 0.14 g/cyl, roughly 4.3 g/s) and closed-loop correction fell to about zero. Had the estimate sat inside the window with the mixture still off, he would have looked at dead time instead, since dead time is a large share of a short idle pulse.
### Reading a saturated trim
On an alpha-N ITB engine closed loop sat at its +20 % clamp with lambda still about 1.2 against a 1.0 target. The fuel the cell lacks is roughly (1 + trim) × (measured / target) = 1.20 × 1.2 = 1.44; the practitioner applied about 1.4 and re-logged. Where trim is not saturated and lambda is on target, the trim alone is the correction (+13 % gave 1.13 on a high-cam pull).
### Charge cooling coefficient step test (G5)
Closed loop off, injector data entered, a steady cell near 2500 to 3000 rpm and 60 kPa (outside the short-pulse region). Tune VE to lambda 1.0, then step the target in that area to 0.90. In a course build the engine reached 0.88: richer than asked, so the coefficient was too high. Halving it from 10 to 5 made lambda track; a technical session on an Evo 9 found the same change, with the default about 1.5 % rich at 0.90. Leaner than asked would mean raising it.
### Dry nitrous stage fuel (`vendor-doc`)
Nitrous flow is roughly 3.6 lb/hr for each rated hp, or the maker's figure, or it can be found by weighing the bottle across a 5 s firing (lb lost × 720 = lb/hr). Fuel lb/hr = nitrous lb/hr ÷ NFR. The Modelled entry in g/s is lb/hr ÷ 7.94; the Traditional entry, %InjDC, is fuel lb/hr ÷ combined injector lb/hr × 100. For a 100 hp stage, 360 lb/hr of nitrous needs 30 lb/hr of fuel at 12:1, which is 3.78 g/s, or about 9.4 % on four 80 lb/hr injectors.
### Rolling launch rpm from speed (`vendor-doc`)
Slip-free engine rpm = road speed (km/h) × wheel speed calibration × un-normalised gear ratio ÷ 360; multiply by (1 + wanted slip). With 25 km/h, calibration 260 and ratio 169.3 this gives about 3057 rpm; 12 % slip (× 1.12) puts that cell of the 3D launch table at about 3424 rpm.
