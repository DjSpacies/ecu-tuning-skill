# Link G4X and G5 notes: limits, boost, idle, throttle and launch

Part of `g4x-g5-notes.md`, which holds the overview, scope and sources; read it first. This file holds the limits, boost, idle, throttle and launch differences on G4X and G5.

## Practical use

### Electronic throttle
- `vendor-doc` Hardware: Monsoon and Storm need an external controller; Xtreme and Fury can use an internal or an external one. Setup mode bypasses all safety checks, so never drive in it.
- `vendor-doc` In mode On, any e-throttle fault disables the throttle and limits the engine to 1800 rpm. Only a power cycle or a mode change resets it; clearing the code in software does not. APS faults close both throttles. In Setup mode faults are shown but nothing shuts down.
- `vendor-doc` Tracking accumulators (TPS against target, TPS main against sub, APS main against sub) climb while the pair is more than 6 % of range apart and fall otherwise; a fault sets at 100 % after the Fault Delay (default 1 s). A duty fault means motor duty has sat on a clamp for longer than the fault delay. Supply faults set when the H-bridge feed is at or below 7.0 V or the 5 V sensor rail is at or below 4.9 V. An accumulator trace shows how close a weak sensor is to tripping. The G4+ 5.6.8 thresholds differ (see Disputed).
- `vendor-doc` When diagnosing, duty stuck on a clamp points to a binding plate or an unplugged motor; target error points to calibration or PID; main/sub tracking points to the sensor, its calibration, or a wrong Sub 100 % value. A CAN-analog APS faults after 2 s without a frame. A CAN or math APS must already span 0 to 100 (send 0 to 1000 with divider 10 for 0.1 %). Fault 120 means the control loop ran late, usually from CPU load caused by too many features. E-throttle and rotary oil pump stepper faults impose engine limits rather than a full kill.
- `vendor-doc` Automatic TPS calibration error numbers: 1, 2, 3 and 14 mean the relay, TPS main, TPS sub or H-bridge output is not assigned; 5 means TPS main does not move; 6 and 7 mean main or sub travel is below 1 V; 10 and 11 mean an input is faulted; 15 means the H-bridge is misconfigured; 16 means reversed (rest is closer to open than to closed, so swap the motor wires or invert the pair's first aux); 17 means the engine is running. The G4+ help floor for travel is 1.5 V.
- `vendor-doc` Tuning settings: deadband defaults to 0 and applies to P and I only; Quiet Throttle halts control at 0 rpm; internal H-bridge PWM is usually 500 to 1000 Hz. A feed-forward table (the duty that holds each position) improves response near the sprung rest point; its values change between engine running and engine stopped because battery voltage changes.
- `vendor-doc` Help starting points. Bosch 54/74/82 mm body: 10000 Hz, P 4.0, I 0.08, D 50 (main about 0.55 rising to 4.4 V, sub about 4.44 falling to 0.59 V; on the matching pedal, sub is about half of main). Toyota clutched body (3S-GTE, 1UZ-FE): 500 Hz, D 60, I 0.121, P 8.0, duty clamps ±95 %, deadband 0.2 %, and the clutch on a native aux as a 250 Hz test PWM fed from switched 12 V. The character is the same as on G4+: large D, tiny I.
- `vendor-doc` From G4X 6.16.15 the transition from e-throttle idle to the target table is smoothed, so the target table at 0 % APS should hold 0 %; any value there adds to the idle opening. This differs from the G4+ advice (see Disputed). PCLink 6.20.37 raised the e-throttle idle clamp maximum to 20 %.
### Limits and engine protection
- `vendor-doc` Progressive cut works as on G4+: a range below the limit, Start Cut interpolated on throttle, and an optional hard limit beyond. Typical values: rpm range 200, MAP range 10 kPa, TPS Low 15 %, Start Cut 30 % at TPS Low and 60 % at TPS 100, End Cut 90 %. If Start Cut at TPS Low is above Start Cut at TPS 100, the larger value is used. If rpm overshoots and then settles at WOT, raise Start Cut (TPS 100).
- `vendor-doc` Cut effect: Adaptive randomises which cylinders are cut so that no cylinder runs too many times in a row; Constant uses a fixed pattern. Typical values: limit ignition trim −5 deg; cut and trim decay 500 ms; limit fuel trim 0 % (offered with ignition-cut modes only). With Advanced mode off, generic defaults are forced.
- `vendor-doc` RPM and MAP limits each have dual tables (switched by a DI or virtual aux), with the current limit and the active table available as runtimes.
- `vendor-doc` There are two speed limits, each with a driving or driven source and each enabled by a switch; when both are active the lower one applies. A typical use is a pit limiter (example 60 km/h).
- `vendor-doc` Throttle safety switch (snowmobiles and similar), digital mode: TPS at or above 8 % while the lever switch is inactive kills the engine.
- `vendor-doc` Fault 68, low oil pressure (from G4X 6.31.2 and G5 7.8.2): the Oil Pressure Switch status reads pressure low, and the fault requires that status plus run time beyond its startup lockout plus rpm at or above a minimum. The status may come from logic (a math block, a GP output, or a virtual aux fed by a sensor) instead of a switch. Confirm any trip on a gauge.
- `vendor-doc` Cruise control needs working driving wheel speed, a set switch, a brake switch (with no Brake NC or NO assigned it never enables) and, on manuals, a clutch switch. Brake, clutch, cancel, or speed outside the min/max lockouts returns Active to Enabled. It has speed mode only and uses a PID with a maximum acceleration; analog switch voltages are matched within a tolerance band (example ±0.3 V).
### Boost control
- `vendor-doc` Closed-loop stages per the help: stage 1 is a fixed spool duty; stage 2 (PD) starts at target − Stage 2 ON; stage 3 (PI) starts after pressure reaches target − Stage 3 ON and the Stage 3 delay has passed. Control drops back to stage 2 when pressure falls a further Stage 3 Hysteresis lower (target 200, ON 20, hysteresis 15 → exit below 165 kPa). Base DC Mode either freezes base duty at its last stage-2 value or keeps it updating in stage 3. Stage 3 ON must be close to target yet reachable, or stage 3 never starts. Training says otherwise (see Disputed).
- `vendor-doc` Gain units: P 1.0 gives 0.5 % duty per kPa of error; I 1.0 gives a 25 %/s ramp per kPa of error; D 1.0 gives 1 % duty per kPa/ms of error change. The integral clamp limits I in both directions, in % duty. The G4+ entries state no units, so do not assume G4+ gains carry over.
- `vendor-doc` Setup guide values not already in `semantics-control.md`: activation at about 80 kPa and 2000 rpm; Stage 2 ON 50 kPa; Stage 3 ON 15 kPa; Stage 3 delay 0.5 s; hysteresis 10 kPa; deactivation timeout 0 s; and an IAT wastegate trim that pulls duty when cold (about −12 % at 0 C, then −8, −6, −4, −2, reaching 0 at 50 C), because dense cold air gives more boost per unit of duty. Base duty is found row by row (every target at 180 kPa abs, then tune; then 200; then 220) before the gains are touched.
- `vendor-doc` Wastegate table axes: open loop uses rpm × TPS (part-throttle cells must not request full boost, or rising pre-throttle pressure takes torque control away from the driver); closed loop uses rpm × boost target.
- `vendor-doc` Trims: wastegate trims (ECT, IAT, gear, GP) scale base duty by a percentage (−50 % of 50 gives 25; +10 % of 50 gives 55); target trims add kPa (200 + 10 gives 210). Targets are in absolute MAP. In Interpolate mode a ratio table blends tables 1 and 2 (0 % = table 1); with switched tables the highest number wins.
- `vendor-doc` Activation needs both the RPM and the MAP threshold; the deactivation timeout keeps control (or stages 2/3) active for a moment after dropping below them. Boost control's own MAP Limit applies full bleed, held for a reactivation delay, independent of the cut-based MAP limit; 0 disables it.
- `vendor-doc` Closed loop on MAP has trouble at part throttle because the throttle drops pressure; where one is fitted, a pre-throttle (turbo outlet) sensor works better as the boost source.
- `vendor-doc` Link/MAC solenoids saturate (fully open) from about 77 % duty, so higher duty only winds up the loop. The max clamp is typically 40 to 85 %; use a min clamp only where a solenoid misbehaves at low duty (Link 3-port: 0 %). PWM is usually 20 Hz (dome solenoids 30 Hz), and voltage compensation is referenced to 14 V.
- `vendor-doc` Dome pressure: a CO2 or similar high-pressure source feeds the wastegate dome through increase and decrease solenoids. Positive PID output drives the increase valve and negative output drives the decrease valve, each at |duty|; an output of 0 shuts both. The gains are faster spool and wider, higher reachable targets; the costs are hardware and harder tuning. The dome acts like added spring (spring 10 psi + dome 20 psi gives about 30 psi boost) but is non-linear at the extremes; confirm on the engine.
- `vendor-doc` Dome plumbing: put the sensor at or close to the dome, not at the solenoid tee (which is noisier). Size the regulator at about 1.5 to 2 × the highest dome pressure needed. Shop air saves CO2 during tuning. With RPM and MAP activation at 0, the dome loop can be tuned with the engine off. Twin gates: tee the line to both domes and sense one. A gauge or an absolute dome sensor are both fine, provided the target uses the same scale.
- `vendor-doc` Dome start values: ramp 800 kPa/s, deadband 0, clamps 0 to 70 %, an imported deadtime table matching the CO2 pressure, integral clamp 5 %, P 0.2, D 0, I 0. On a time plot, step the target between about 20 % and 80 % of max dome pressure (between 100 and 400 kPa for a 500 kPa max). Increase P until it snaps onto target without oscillation, then add a little I, and D only against overshoot spikes. Short dome spikes are fine because they never reach the manifold.
- `vendor-doc` Dome extras: the ramp rate limits torque rise and dome overshoot. Deadband closes both valves near target (less gas used, more error), and status shows Deadband. Solenoid deadtime matters because dome duties are small, so output duty deviates slightly from WGate DC. For third-party valves, ramp the target slowly with P only: with deadtime short the dome lags with high duty; with it long the duty is jittery, with continual fill and dump; with it right the dome tracks at about 1 to 2 % duty. Help test: 0 ms lagged badly; 4 ms about 3 %; 4.2 ms about 1 % (correct); 5 ms jitter. Prefer slightly short, and redo it at every operating voltage.
### Idle control
- `vendor-doc` Position = base + load offsets + closed-loop correction, clamped to min/max even when table values lie outside. The closed-loop deadband extends half its value either side of target. In open-loop mode the Idle RPM table is used only by idle ignition control. Vendor opinion: open loop is enough for most cars, and closed loop over a solid open-loop base idles most reliably. Start the integral gain at 1.
- `vendor-doc` Offsets and idle-ups never add up: only the single largest active offset and the largest active idle-up apply. With the fan already on, A/C contributes nothing if the fan offset is bigger, so size each offset for the heaviest load acting alone.
- `vendor-doc` The A/C offset is driven by the A/C request switch, not the clutch output, so extra air arrives before the clutch engages. The clutch engages only above 400 rpm and drops out above the TPS, APS, MAP or injector duty lockouts; Full mode also uses refrigerant pressure and evaporator temperature lockouts.
- `vendor-doc` The startup offset is held and then decayed from first engine speed. The fan offset adds an extra initial step for motor spin-up current. Dashpot is an offset that is held and then decayed, applied on entry after a lockout. Idle-ups (raised targets) require closed loop. Automatics have a 3D gear/drive offset with wheel speed as one axis, for converter creep load.
- `vendor-doc` Idle lockouts: speed (wheel, GPS, or In Driving Gear where no speed input exists), APS with e-throttle or TPS otherwise, MAP, and rpm. The rpm lockout is an offset above target in closed loop or with idle ignition, and an absolute rpm in open loop without idle ignition. A speed lockout allows aggressive idle tuning without spoiling driveability.
- `vendor-doc` Clamps apply to idle control only: the anti-lag ISC override may exceed them, and with e-throttle the pedal overrides them once idle is locked out.
- `vendor-doc` E-throttle idle: the position covers the full plate travel, so small steps swing rpm hard. Base, offsets and clamps are far smaller than for a valve, with Max Clamp at about 10 % or below. The idle position merges into the pedal target, handing over slightly above the idle position to avoid undershoot.
- `vendor-doc` Idle ignition is a PD controller around an idle ignition target (a single value or 2D, for example hot/cold), clamped to min/max, sharing the ISC lockouts, and best paired with closed-loop air. It replaces the G4+ rpm-error table (PID against PD is under Disputed in `g4x-g5-ignition.md`).
- `vendor-doc` Closed-loop idle hunting may come from large timing differences between adjacent low rpm/load cells, so keep timing smooth around idle.
- `vendor-doc` Idle load fuel trims (power steering, A/C, fan, drive, GP idle input) add a fixed ms to pulse width while the condition and idle control are active; with idle control off they apply only under 2000 rpm. Any gear except 0, −, N and P counts as driving unless the clutch or neutral/park switches indicate otherwise.
- `vendor-doc` Idle Status sequence: startup position → startup decay → dashpot hold → dashpot decay → Active, or RPM Target when inside the deadband. Hold and RPM Lockout mean idle control is not running. ISC Override means anti-lag or cyclic idle has the actuator. Idle Base Position is the value before the closed-loop, fan, startup and A/C terms are added.
- `vendor-doc` Stepper hold duty is about 20 to 30 % to avoid overheating. Key-On Fuel Lockout reset allows no fuel or spark (prime aside) while the stepper homes; it is for valves large enough to flare the engine.
### Launch, anti-lag and cyclic idle
- `vendor-doc` Launch Active requires the activation input on for the arming time, throttle above TPS Activation, and driven wheel speed below Disarming Speed. Launch trims apply only then. Launch Limit reads 0 while launch is off or the input is inactive.
- `vendor-doc` The launch ignition trim needs both its TP and its rpm threshold exceeded. Normal mode decays to zero across the decay time after exit; Degrees Absolute forces the value (−35 = 35 ATDC) and snaps back to the main map at exit, with no decay.
- `vendor-doc` Latched Launch RPM (roll racing) latches the limit to the rpm present when the switch is pressed (after the arming time) and holds it until release.
- `vendor-doc` A 3D launch rpm on vehicle speed acts as rolling traction control (the calculation is in the Worked example of `g4x-g5-notes.md`).
- `vendor-doc` Anti-lag entry needs it armed (input, virtual aux or Always On), rpm above AL Enable RPM, and TP(Main) above AL Enable TPS. It stays active while either remains above, and exits only after both have been below for the Deactivation Timeout (in effect, spool retention); losing the arm input ends it immediately. TPS hysteresis is added going in and subtracted coming out (70 % ± 10 % → on at 80, off at 60). Help examples: enable at 3000 rpm and 60 % TP. Very low enable values (500 rpm, 0 % TP) leave only the switch in charge and keep anti-lag on at low throttle, the harshest configuration.
- `vendor-doc` AL retard modes: Degrees adds to present timing (a negative value retards by that amount, not to it); Degrees Absolute replaces timing, with a 0 cell reverting to normal timing (so 0 BTDC is unavailable), as on G4+. AL fuel trim is in % of base pulse width. The mode list is disputed (see Disputed).
- `vendor-doc` AL fuel cut is the % of cylinders cut (typically 50 %). Each cut event pushes a cylinder's worth of cool air through, which helps EGT under heavy retard; raise fuel cut if anti-lag EGT is excessive. The AL ignition cut table works only with AL Ign Cut Table enabled. Retard, fuel cut and ignition cut each have a switchable second table (wet/dry, for example).
- `vendor-doc` ISC override: the anti-lag and cyclic idle tables drive the idle actuator directly, past the ISC clamps, provided idle control is configured. Factory idle valves rarely flow enough, so plan a bigger bypass valve. With e-throttle the override table alone positions the throttle (about 20 to 25 % at 0 % pedal, up to 100 % at full pedal), which makes cyclic idle redundant.
- `vendor-doc` Cyclic idle is a fuel-cut sequence that limits idle rpm while the throttle is held cracked (as racing rules require) with anti-lag off; it depends on the normal RPM limit being set up. A typical limit is about 1800 rpm; 0 means no limiting. Cool Down mode continues it for a set time after anti-lag stops. A controlled bypass or e-throttle makes it unnecessary.
### Traction, torque and power management
- `vendor-doc` Traction control acts only through torque reduction requests to Torque Management, so it does nothing unless that is on (status Off: Torque Module). The slip threshold table is both the intervention point and the closed-loop target; the overlay multiplies it (50 % slip with a 3 % overlay gives 51.5 %). It is locked out below rpm, throttle and driven-speed thresholds, and is absent on G4X Atom, Monsoon and Storm. Reduce-recover-reduce pulsing usually means the P/D gains are wrong; tune them in a repeatable situation with Slip logged, and set Torque Recovery Rate.
- `vendor-doc` Torque management prerequisites: a finished tune, Modelled fuel, Load = MAP, and accurate injector data plus MAP, fuel pressure and lambda calibrations, because Estimated Torque derives from modelled air and fuel mass.
- `vendor-doc` Estimated Torque (Nm) is Load(Abs) in excess of the Load(Abs) at Idle setting, × Estimated Torque Multiplier, minus allowances for limit ignition trim and % fuel/ignition cut. It reads 0 with torque management off, and it is meaningless until the idle load (Park/Neutral for automatics) and the multiplier are matched to dyno torque. It assumes MBT timing, so the predicted effect of retard is weaker on knock-limited cells.
- `vendor-doc` Calibration steps: tune first with torque management off and APS indexing the throttle target; enable it; read warm-idle Load(Abs) off a log into Load(Abs) at Idle; disable cuts and zero the max ignition trim and the boost/throttle trim rates; scale the multiplier until the estimate equals dyno torque (allowing for driveline loss on a chassis or hub dyno); log a WOT sweep for peak estimated torque. Next, set Driver Torque Request on APS (rpm optional) from 0 Nm at 0 % up to peak at 100 %. Overall Torque Request (about 8 evenly spaced breakpoints up to peak) replaces APS on the boost and e-throttle target axes; on the dyno, hold each cell and adjust throttle until the estimate matches the request. Help example: about 19.5 % throttle reached max torque at 1000 rpm, and no rpm needed more than about 83.3 %.
- `vendor-doc` Driver Torque Request options: a single table; 2 or 3 switched tables (3 is better than 2); additive; or table 2 as a ceiling. The shape is a matter of preference (small NA engines often climb steeply over the first 20 % or so of pedal, strong ones more linearly), but keep it smooth.
- `vendor-doc` Overall Torque Request is the driver request, floored at the largest minimum request (itself at least 0 Nm) and capped at the lowest maximum request (DSC, transmission, driver − reduction, driver × (1 − reduction %), traction output); maximums override minimums. A DSC or transmission maximum of 0 is ignored, and 1 Nm is the smallest effective value. DSC and transmission requests are absolute; Torque Reduction and Torque Reduction % are relative to Driver Torque Request, so a request table set above true estimated torque puts the reduction above real output, where it achieves nothing. Torque increases (TCU or DSC minimum requests, downshift blips) work only with Overall Torque Request driving the throttle and boost targets.
- `vendor-doc` Actuation sequence: ignition retard comes first, up to the max ignition trim, then cuts. In parallel, the closed-loop boost target is trimmed until wastegate duty bottoms out, and only after that does the e-throttle close. When torque is met, cuts and retard withdraw and throttle plus boost trim maintain it. Without e-throttle and closed-loop boost, retard and cuts remain for the whole event. Every actuator (limit ignition trim, cut, boost target trim, e-throttle trim) has rates.
- `vendor-doc` Power management compares wheel speed, VSS or driveshaft rpm (GP RPM input) against a target table, normally on race time, and retards and/or cuts when ahead of target. Tied to the race timer, it runs only while the timer is running or paused. On Delay (example 0.20 s) keeps it off the launch, and max gear and max speed lockouts set to 0 are disabled. Positive ignition trim means advance.
- `vendor-doc` Power management tables: use a small deadband, retard before cut, and cut only for big errors, rarely beyond about 30 %. Driveshaft example: 0 up to 50 rpm over; 10 deg retard at 100 over and 30 deg at 200 over; cut starting beyond 200 over, 10 % at 300 and 30 % at 500; as much as 5 deg advance when 200 to 300 rpm under late in a run. With wet nitrous or cut-sensitive engines, zero the cut tables (backfire risk). Open-loop cut/trim tables on any axes (race time, ride height, etc.) anticipate known slip points.
- `vendor-doc` The race timer starts when its input is released (transbrake, two-step, clutch) or pressed. A pause input stops it counting; without a reset input it resets once its input returns to the pre-start state; reaching Max Time shows Finished and also ends power management. Set Max Time slightly longer than the longest run.
### Gear shift control
- `vendor-doc` The shift pipeline runs request validation → lockouts → shift-type classification → staged torque actions → re-activation timeout, so diagnose by stage. Gear Shift Status reports the blocking lockout (user, clutch, speed, compressor pressure, end of gears, rpm low, re-activation timeout, TPS/APS low on upshift, per-gear max downshift rpm) or the phase (limiter hold, torque reintroduction, mis-shift wait).
- `vendor-doc` Driven or overrun is decided by APS/TPS against the Drive/Overrun threshold. With only an upshift input, or with H-pattern lever force, the ECU assumes up = driven and down = overrun, so a lift-off upshift gets a downshift blip; fit separate up and down inputs.
- `vendor-doc` A driven upshift runs dog unload (time, cut and trims per gear), then the main shift (fixed cut, or RPM Match Limit cuts with shaft speed matching on; ended by time, input release, or the barrel reaching the next gear within the min/max durations), then linear torque reintroduction. Where dog-unload and match-limit cuts overlap, the larger applies. Overrun upshifts batter the dogs, so use the clutch.
- `vendor-doc` Overrun shifts begin with a blip (e-throttle target, idle target or solenoid) limited by Blip Duration; shaft speed matching cuts during the blip turn it into closed-loop rev matching. Driven downshifts unload and blip at the same time, then the optional Limiter Hold resists the push-on from blip air.
- `vendor-doc` The shaft speed matching limiter looks up cut from rpm above target (example: 200 rpm over gives 60 % cut). The help advises leaving it on in most cases, for more consistent shifts and less dog wear.
- `vendor-doc` The RPM target is computed from the ratio change alone or with driving wheel speed as well (unloaded wheels on stands can send the blip target very high). Adjust scales the change: going from 4000 to a calculated 3000, 50 % gives 3500 and 150 % gives 2500. Start at about 105 % for upshifts and 95 % for downshifts, leaving some cut for a smooth ramp.
- `vendor-doc` Blip End Mode: Normal (ends with the stage), RPM Target, or Predictive (ends early by rpm rate × response time, typically 40 to 80 ms). Driver-shifted boxes suit RPM Target or Predictive; paddles suit Gear Barrel Position.
- `vendor-doc` The next gear counts as reached when barrel voltage is within the margin of that gear's voltage (0.1 V margin, first 1 V, second 2 V → second above 1.9 V). This margin is separate from the gear detection tolerance and usually tighter. Calibrate each gear voltage fully engaged.
- `vendor-doc` Typical starting values: validation 5 to 20 ms (short gives false shifts, long gives delay); TPS lockout 20 % for upshift-low and paddle downshift-high; dog unload 10 to 20 ms at 90 % cut; pneumatic paddle cut delay 40 ms. With anti-lag, use APS for the downshift lockout, since anti-lag keeps the throttle open. Ignition cut is quicker but some engines dislike it (backfire); fuel cut is softer and slower.
- `vendor-doc` Paddles: the actuator fires after its delay, in parallel with the torque stages. A healthy barrel sensor gives a closed-loop main shift that times out to a MisShift count and a retry; a faulted sensor gives a timed fallback. Climbing MisShift counts mean the barrel is late into the next gear (actuator pressure, gearbox, margins). Set the dog unload cut delay from a log (actuator on to first barrel movement). Begin with zero stacked downshifts.
- `vendor-doc` Paddle lockouts: per-gear max downshift rpm (the typical reason high-rpm downshifts are refused), compressor pressure, and neutral/reverse rules. The compressor cycles between target and target − hysteresis; its startup delay counts from above 400 rpm, and with the startup and rpm lockouts at 0 it runs with the engine stopped.
- `vendor-doc` H-pattern gearbox with a clutch switch: the shift ends by Digital Input or by Time. Raising the upshift-low TPS lockout (example 80 %) limits flat shifts to full power; dipping below it mid-shift gives TPS Low Lockout rather than a cut. A lever strain gauge (about 2.5 V at rest on 0-5 V) sees all four shift types but requires the timed end mode.
### Automatic transmission control
- `vendor-doc` Built-in TCU modes: Jatco 4-speed (Nissan RE4R0xA; phase-1 Subaru 4EAT); Toyota A340E family (A340E, A341E, A343E, A241E); GM 4L60E family (60/65/70/75E); and GM 4L80E/85E, which the help reckons also suits 4T40E and Ford 4R70W. Outputs are Gear, Target Gear and Max Gear; the Auto shifter modes output Gear Shifter Position.
- `vendor-doc` Shifter decoding. In Auto Digital the first active input found when scanning slots A→J wins, so list order is priority. Place P, R and N above the manual buttons, gears that allow manual below M+/M−, and full M just above D so that manual is only reachable from D. D may be the final entry, set Always On, when the box wires only L, 2 and O/D off (typical of Toyota A34xE/A241E). Auto GM 3 Switch cannot tell P from N (it shows N), and manual inputs work only in 2, 3 and D. In Auto Analog the voltage windows must not overlap.
- `vendor-doc` Shift tables come in up/down pairs per gear, with selectable load. A cell where both fire does nothing, so upshift speed must be at or above downshift speed everywhere. Interpolate mode blends two sets by ratio (25 % between 20 and 40 km/h gives 25 km/h). Manual mode ignores the tables.
- `vendor-doc` Shift completion by slip: slip against the old and new ratios is recorded at shift start, and the shift is finished once the remaining progress falls to the setting (e.g. 20 %), bounded by per-shift min/max duration tables. Help case: a 2-3 shift with ratios 1.5 → 1.0, engine at 3000 rpm and output at 1667 rpm starts with slip of 20 % on the old ratio and 80 % on the new; it is done once slip is 32 %. Shift duration feeds shifting line pressure, torque reduction, accumulator duty, GM 3-2 timing and forced lockup release.
- `vendor-doc` The upshift limiter applies cut (a table on rpm error to the predicted post-shift rpm), retard and fuel trim between its start % and shift end. Start it only once slip begins falling; cutting during the flat hydraulic delay just loses drive. A min speed lockout avoids distorted slip right after launch. Shifting Max Torque limits estimated torque per shift type after a delay, and requires torque management.
- `vendor-doc` Force upshift: rpm over its table value for the delay selects the next gear (delay 0 = off). Max downshift rpm blocks downshifts, judged on pre-shift rpm. A downshift request at the same time as a force upshift gives neither, so force upshift does not guarantee protection when the shift tables conflict.
- `vendor-doc` Manual: pressing M+ or M− while outside M keeps the chosen gear for the Re-Activation Timeout, with Active Shift Table reading Manual Control, and for longer after M− while the pedal stays under Coast APS/TPS. Allow RPM Forced Upshift keeps rpm-forced upshifts in manual. Force First Gear fires once as speed drops past its threshold, and re-arms only after speed rises again.
- `vendor-doc` Lockup is inhibited while shifting or in the re-activation timeout; in non-forward gears; below min gear, below transmission temperature, or below per-gear speed (lock and unlock rows are separate); with APS/TPS out of limits; above max load; above max slip; and until everything has cleared for the validation time. When locked, a lockout that arises mid-locking unlocks immediately, shifts unlock instantly (a positive TC shift delay unlocks before the shift, a negative one after), and the remaining lockouts ramp out. High APS/TPS and max load force unlock; low APS/TPS and cold transmission do not. Set the locked max-load limit at or above the unlocked one, or lockup cycles. Force-disable outranks force-enable and dyno mode.
- `vendor-doc` Dyno Mode holds the target gear and locks the converter above a set speed, overriding most lockup lockouts. It still obeys Max Gear, shifter position, the forward-gear rule, the unlock-during-shift rule and Force Enable/Disable, and it still upshifts through the gears to reach the target.
- `vendor-doc` PWM lockup ramps from Start Locking to Hold duty, holds, then steps to Locked; the GM PWM-plus-switched mode adds 0 % periods on each side. Help duties: Jatco/4EAT 50 Hz, about 5 % unlocked, 30 % start, 95 % locked; 4L60E 32 Hz, unlocked about 90 % above 13 km/h, start 25 %, hold and locked 50 % (measured on a VS Commodore: about 95 % locked, min speed 30 km/h); 4L80E 32 Hz, 0 / 30 / 60 / 70 %. A locked converter alone does not keep engine speed up on lift-off; the overrun clutch does.
- `vendor-doc` Line pressure works by bleed: more duty gives more bleed and so less pressure, an assumption the closed-loop PID keeps even with inverted output polarity. Higher pressure gives firmer shifts and more torque capacity; too low causes clutch slip and wear. Open loop uses a duty table plus a temperature offset. Closed loop (preferred) adds a PID on a pressure sensor toward a target table. Shifting tables apply during the Shifting state (see Disputed in `g4x-g5-fuel.md`). On the Subaru 4EAT type, keep a min duty clamp so fluid always bleeds, otherwise the overrun clutch fails to work.
- `vendor-doc` The overrun clutch keeps engine speed from falling below output shaft speed (engine braking on lift) and is the counterpart to lockup. Not all boxes overrun in every gear (Subaru BG 4EAT, Patrol RE4R03A: not 1st in D). Jatco mode disables it for 200 ms on a 3-2 downshift with the pedal over Max APS/TPS; the GM 4L60E family uses this function for its 3-2 solenoid; the 4L80E lacks one.
- `vendor-doc` Shift accumulator (Toyota JZS161 A340E): less bleed gives firmer shifts. The standby duty is fixed outside shifts (about 15 % typical) and comes from a table during them. Transmission temperature is computed even with automatic control off.
- `vendor-doc` Speed source: output shaft speed is preferred, ideally via GP RPM, over transfer-case or wheel speed when there is a high/low range or tyre and diff ratios vary. Never use a wheel speed that has an alternate calibration switch, since switching would alter the shift tune. Subaru 4EAT cluster speed originates at the rear driveshaft and departs from output shaft speed under slip; cluster signals read down to zero but disappear if the cluster is removed; reluctor output-shaft sensors may drop out at crawl speed. Help pulse counts per rev: Patrol RE4R03A 21; VS 4L60E 40 teeth; A340E 4.
- `vendor-doc` Solenoid drive: Jatco/4EAT and A340E shift solenoids, plus several lockup and line pressure solenoids, are high-side switched while ECU outputs are low side, so use an H-bridge pin, FET, relay or PDM. GM line pressure solenoids (about 3-5 ohm) need a high-current output. Subaru 4EAT line pressure is factory 50 Hz peak-and-hold (15 %, 3 ms on the direct path, then the resistor path; about 4 A peak, 0.875 A hold), reproduced with two PDM outputs on a shared timer; a direct pin alone works, but the solenoid runs hotter.
- `vendor-doc` Help gear ratios, listed from top gear down to first, for checking a table of real ratios: VS 4L60E 0.696/1.000/1.625/3.059; A241E 0.892/1.296/2.008/3.643; A343E 0.753/1.000/1.530/2.804; A341E 0.705/1.000/1.531/2.531; A340E 0.705/1.000/1.531/2.804; Jatco/4EAT 0.694/1.000/1.545/2.785 (R34/C34 code B 0.694/1.000/1.619/3.027). Some transmission pages are flagged untested or carry guessed pinouts.
### Overrun fuel cut
- `vendor-doc` DFCO entry: TP/AP below the threshold (example 5.0 %) and rpm above the deactivation rpm, after a delay (example 0.5 s). APS is used with e-throttle, and an APS/TPS mix with cruise. DFCO needs a calibrated TPS.
- `vendor-doc` DFCO is blocked during overrun upshifts and downshifts (since G4X 6.19.37), with anti-lag armed and active (since G4X 6.17.8), and whenever Overall / Combined Minimum Torque Request is above 0 Nm (G4X 6.31.2 / G5 7.8.2), for example on a transmission blip request. Missing DFCO on such a car may therefore be by design.
- `vendor-doc` Ignition ramps out before the fuel cut and back in after fuel returns (example −10 deg across 2 s). A 3D deactivation rpm table ought to rise at low coolant temperature, because cold idle targets are higher and cold engines handle cut poorly.
### Nitrous control
- `vendor-doc` A dedicated nitrous function (G4+ relied on dual tables) offers spool stage 0 plus up to 3 stages, each Wet, Dry, Wet Progressive or Dry Progressive. Fuel trim is a constant fuel flow, not a % of base pulse: g/s in the Modelled modes and %InjDC (% of one injector's max flow) in Traditional. On a four-stroke, 10 %InjDC is about 4 ms/cycle at 3000 rpm and 2 ms/cycle at 6000, which is about 60 cc/min per cylinder with 600 cc/min injectors whatever the rpm. Fuel, ignition and lambda trims add across active stages.
- `vendor-doc` Mass-based NFR starting points: methanol 6.4:1; E100 7.2:1; E85 7.9:1; gasoline 12:1. US kit makers tend to give density-blind gasoline-equivalent ratios, so their numbers differ; Modelled mode accounts for density by itself. Dry-stage sizing is in the Worked example of `g4x-g5-notes.md`.
- `vendor-doc` Solenoids are non-linear below about 15 % and above about 90 % duty (count 90 % as fully open). A 0 % duty cell closes the solenoid but leaves the fuel and ignition trims on their own tables, so place solenoid duty on the fuel table axis to make fuel follow commanded nitrous. Progressive duty is compensated to 14.0 V.
- `vendor-doc` On and off delays ramp the trims linearly, in seconds or engine cycles (cycles are more uniform over rpm). A rich spike at stage entry on a dry kit means fuel is arriving ahead of nitrous, so lengthen the fuel on-delay; off-delays cover nitrous still in the manifold.
- `vendor-doc` Above nitrous Max RPM all solenoids shut and the trims fall away; set it slightly under the rev limit so the manifold is clear before any cut (backfire danger with a wet charge). Stages can be disabled during any cut, which lets a spool stage coexist with launch.
- `vendor-doc` Stage lambda trim moves the target, and so adds fuel, only in a Modelled mode or with open-loop lambda correction. Non-numeric gears count as gear 0, and gear detection may read 0 mid-shift, so back up min-gear lockouts with another lockout, race time for instance. Global lockouts: bottle pressure (needs its sensor), ECT window, max rpm and enable input.

## Disputed / conflicts

- **MAP-to-baro tolerance.** Training quoted about 5 kPa in one session as the most the calibration absorbs; another session recalled 1 to 2 kPa, with doubt. Help 7.8.2 (MAP Sensor Calibration) refuses calibration above a 5 kPa difference. The help governs, and 1 to 2 kPa is not a limit. (The G4+ "within 1 kPa" figure is post-calibration agreement, which is a different thing.)
- **Quick Tune.** The practitioner says Quick Tune needs a controlled environment where a cell can be held, so it is impractical on the road; in another session he reports two or three Quick Tune drive cycles leaving little correction. His stated preference is manual log review, or the mixture map fed with an uncorrected-lambda math channel. Help 7.8.2 says a write needs the operating point held near cell centre for the stabilise time, which supports the controlled-environment view, though road use is not forbidden.
- **Idle Target Error sign.** Help topics differ: the Idle Status runtime gives target − actual, while the Idle Speed Control runtimes give engine speed − target (positive = idling high). The G4+ idle ignition axis reads positive as rpm under target. Establish the sign from a log first.
- **Boost stage content and latching.** Training (Setup checks): stage 2 is base duty and stage 3 is PID; with only stage 2 allowed, duty latched (workaround: stages 2+3 with zero gains). Help 7.8.2 (Boost PID Setup - Stages): stage 2 is PD and stage 3 is PI, and Base DC Mode chooses between base duty frozen at the last stage-2 value and base duty updated continuously. The latch seen may be Base DC Mode set to freeze; test by toggling it with zero gains.
- **Stage 3 hysteresis.** The Closed Loop Boost Setup Guide gives 10 kPa, while Boost PID Setup - Stages gives 15 kPa as "typical". Both are only starting points.
- **Boost cut relative to target.** Training: an overboost cut needs closed loop, so that there is a target to compare with. The Help 7.8.2 topics seen show two MAP limits (the engine-protection table, and boost control's full-bleed MAP Limit), each against fixed values. They are not shown to contradict (a target-relative cut may be documented elsewhere); verify in software before relying on either.
- **Two solenoids under closed loop.** Training: closed loop cannot span two solenoids (top/bottom port gate). Help: dome mode runs one PID over increase and decrease solenoids. The two are compatible if the training claim is limited to conventional dual-port plumbing.
- **Anti-lag retard modes.** G4+ training (`semantics-control.md`): relative, percentage, absolute. G4X/G5 records (AL Ign Retard Mode): Degrees and Degrees Absolute only. Whether percentage mode is really missing is unconfirmed; read the tune's mode before interpreting AL retard values.
- **E-throttle target at zero pedal.** G4+ 5.6.8 help: about 2.5 % at zero pedal near idle rpm as an idle aid, and 0 % in mid-rpm overrun. G4X 6.16.15 notes and the 7.8.2 target table topic: with e-throttle running idle, 0 % APS should give 0 %, since anything else adds to the idle opening. Do not port a G4+ zero-pedal row to G4X.
- **E-Throttle Target runtime.** G4+ 5.6.8: the target is base plus corrections such as the idle offset, so the difference shows idle's share. On G4X/G5 7.8.2, during e-throttle idle the plate tracks the idle target while the runtime still shows table plus trims. Keep both version-scoped.
- **E-throttle fault thresholds.** G4+ 5.6.8 help: plate beyond ±2 % of target; AP main/sub over 3.0 % for 2 s (code 77); 5 V rail below 4.85 V; CAN APS stale after 1 s; a 1.5 V floor for auto TPS calibration; rev limit 1800 (or 1800 to 2000 rpm, varying by topic). G4X/G5 7.8.2: a 6 % accumulator threshold on all three pairs; 5 V rail at or below 4.9 V; CAN APS stale after 2 s; calibration floor 1 V; 1800 rpm. Keep each version's numbers to its own logs.
- **Gear ratio table units.** G4+ 5.6.8: Gear Ratio Calc gives rpm / Hz. G4X/G5 7.8.2: rpm per km/h scaled by Gear Normalise Ratio, or real ratios when the source is shaft rpm; auto-calculation needs at least 3 gears.
- **Shift light in neutral.** G4+ help: gear 1 settings are used in neutral. G4X/G5 7.8.2: gear 0 settings are used when no gear is detected.
- **Internal MAP boost range on JZX90.** The JZX90 board switch page gives 7 bar absolute as about 3 bar boost. The TTLink and DC5 pages give 7.0 bar absolute as about 6 bar boost, and 4.0 bar as about 3. One page is likely mislabelled; read the sticker.
