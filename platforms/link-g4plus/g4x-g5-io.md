# Link G4X and G5 notes: inputs, outputs, logging and CAN

Part of `g4x-g5-notes.md`, which holds the overview, scope and sources; read it first. This file holds the inputs, outputs, logging and CAN differences on G4X and G5.

## Practical use

### Reading I/O, CAN and control channels
- `vendor-doc` In the aux runtimes, duty and frequency stay at 0 for non-PWM functions (on/off, injection, ignition). A fault on Aux 11-16 means open-circuit detection; on other channels it means a hardware fault.
- `vendor-doc` ADIO voltage (G5 Voodoo Neo, Pro V2) clips at 5 V even though the optional pull-up is 12 V, and it reads 0 V when the pin is configured as an output. A reading of 5.00 V on a pulled-up switch may therefore be 12 V, and 0 V on an output is expected; check outputs through their status, duty and frequency instead.
- `vendor-doc` CAN timeouts are 2 s. A timed-out CAN DI goes to Fault; a CAN analog or frequency channel goes to 0 (it is also 0 if nothing has been received since power-up); a CAN thermocouple goes to 0 C and is dropped from the EGT max, min and average. A 0 on CAN-fed ECT, APS or EGT therefore means a missing frame, and any protection keyed to that channel sees a low reading, not a high one.
- `vendor-doc` CAN collisions: if two senders feed one CAN analog, DI or frequency channel, the value flickers between them. The same happens with a dyno wideband on Lambda 1/2 while another device feeds those channels, so route dyno lambda to Lambda 3/4. Lambda 3-12 can only be filled over CAN (the count is disputed; see Disputed).
- `vendor-doc` A dyno can send rpm, per-wheel rpm, kW and Nm over CAN as ECU runtimes, so one log carries both engine and dyno data.
- `vendor-doc` GPS speed depends on fix status. Dead reckoning (no satellites) gives an inertial estimate; a 2D fix ignores elevation; Time Only gives no speed. Check the status before trusting GPS speed in a road log. Even with internal GPS selected, CAN-received GPS can still overwrite the runtimes.
- `vendor-doc` With the e-throttle at idle, the plate follows the idle target, yet E-Throttle Target keeps showing table + trims, so do not work out target error from that pair at idle. Motor duty is positive when opening and negative (−) when closing.
- `vendor-doc` Clutch or converter slip (%) compares engine rpm with the rpm implied by the gear ratio (× gear normalise ratio when the source is road speed); a positive value means the engine is ahead of the driveline. A wrong ratio or normalise value gives false slip, which can trip slip lockouts.
- `vendor-doc` Gear ratio cells hold engine rpm per km/h, multiplied by Gear Normalise Ratio; that ratio can carry the diff ratio and tyre size, so real gearbox ratios can be typed in. With a shaft-rpm source the cells must be true transmission ratios. G4+ help uses rpm per Hz (see `g4x-g5-control.md`, Disputed), so never copy values from one to the other.
- `vendor-doc` In RPM/Speed gear detection with only one gear entered, a computed ratio < 1.1 × the first cell is read as first gear. Misreads are possible with automatic converter slip and with motorcycle slipper clutches on a downshift.
- `vendor-doc` Trim arbitration: between Limit Ignition Trim (limiters, torque management) and Launch Ignition Trim, the larger one applies, not the sum. Limit Fuel Trim takes the largest active or decaying trim among the RPM, MAP and speed limits and both GP RPM limits. % Fuel Cut and % Ignition Cut show the combined result of all active limits, gear shift cuts included.
- `vendor-doc` Limiter states are Inactive, Active, Off, Activation Delay, Cut Decay Time and Startup Lockout. A cut that persists after its cause clears is in the decay phase. The Limits Flags Word (for CAN) uses bit 0 for RPM, 1 for MAP, 2 for speed, 3 for max ignition, 6 for overrun and 7 for traction.
- `vendor-doc` PID logging (boost, idle air, idle ignition, e-throttle, cam banks, fuel pump, cruise, traction) shows feed forward, P, I, D and the integral clamp. A large steady I means the feed forward is wrong; I sitting on the clamp means the controller is saturated; noisy P or D points to signal noise.
- `vendor-doc` Boost channels: Boost Base DC is the wastegate table value before the ECT, IAT, gear and GP trims; Boost Target is 0 in open loop; in dome mode a negative WGate DC means the decrease (bleed) solenoid is active. The help descriptions of Boost Proportional and Boost Derivative look swapped, so trust the channel names.
- `vendor-doc` Shift channels: Gear Shift Count increments once per completed shift, not per paddle retry, so extra counts mean spurious requests. Last Shift Period runs from the start of the shift to torque reintroduction. A blip target of 0 % changes neither the throttle nor the idle target.
- The idle ignition channel on G4X comes from a closed-loop controller with min and max clamps, not from a 3D error table (training calls it PID; `vendor-doc` calls it PD). `vendor-doc`: an Ignition Idle status of Min or Max Ignition means the controller has run out of authority, which points to a wrong idle air base position.
- `vendor-doc` The traction torque limit is visible only as Modified Possible Torque, which is the lower of Estimated Torque and the lowest maximum request.
- `vendor-doc` The CE lamp flashes tens as long flashes and units as short ones. The mask table suppresses chosen codes at the lamp, while PCLink still lists them all. Codes can be cleared from PCLink; through a digital input held ≥ 1 s (this clears the ECU codes and the lamp, but not PCLink's list); or with the key on, engine stopped and TPS (APS with e-throttle) held over 80 % for 10 s.
### Outputs, aux functions and power
- `vendor-doc` Aux low-side ratings are 2 A on G4X (direct loads over about 7 ohm) and 5 A on many G5 pins (over about 3 ohm). Injection and ignition channels used as aux are open collector, with no flywheel diode and no pull-up, so PWM a solenoid on them only with an external diode. Functions are restricted to the channel types that support them (low or high side, PWM, stepper, H-bridge, synchronous).
- `vendor-doc` Aux 5-8 in high-side mode have no PWM and a tiny current limit, so they are unsuitable for transmission shift or line pressure solenoids; low is floating and high is 12 V.
- `vendor-doc` Plug-in boards that turn an aux into a high-side output (CivicLink 95 Aux 5/6 or MX5 NB2 Aux 1, for example) do so through an inverting FET. Set the channel to low side in PCLink; the header pin goes high when the internal drive pulls low. Polarity that looks backwards on such pins can therefore be correct.
- `vendor-doc` The aux test can force an output on, force it off, or run a 50 % square wave at a chosen frequency. Aux 11-16 also detect an open circuit.
- `vendor-doc` GP outputs combine as many as three comparisons (<, >, =, <=, >=, not equal) with AND or OR, plus an on delay and an off delay (the conditions must hold continuously for each). G4+ has only a switch-off timer. Examples are VTEC on above about 4500 rpm with a brief off delay, and nitrous arming from gear, MAP, an rpm window and a switch.
- `vendor-doc` There are four GP timers with 1 ms resolution and a maximum adjustable to 16,000,000 s. Reset modes: held at zero while the activation input is inactive, held at zero while it is active, or reset at every change of state without holding. G4+ timers run in 2 ms steps up to 60 s.
- `vendor-doc` A virtual aux is aux logic without a pin. A CAN aux adds duty and frequency, so the ECU can compute a PWM function (boost, idle, GP PWM) that another device, such as a Link PDM, executes over CAN. Complex logic is usually built as a GP output feeding a virtual aux (for example a CE lamp combined with temperature and knock warnings, or compound log start conditions).
- `vendor-doc` GP Rotary Switch maps an analog voltage to evenly spaced integer positions (set by the count, min and max V and the lowest position number), with an error value on a fault or CAN timeout. When it is used as an axis, the integers stop a noisy switch voltage from blending between columns.
- `vendor-doc` GP 3 Wire Solenoid (from G4X 6.25.5 / G5 7.2.5) drives Aux 1/2 or Aux 3/4 as a complementary pair, so a 3-wire rotary valve can serve jobs other than idle. Only one instance is available.
- `vendor-doc` GP H-bridge (from G4X 6.28.1 / G5 7.5.1) drives an OE electric wastegate motor (through the boost function), variable valve lift, a throttle body acting as a blow-off valve, or open/closed devices such as intake flaps.
  It has a single analog feedback and fewer safeguards than the e-throttle, so never use it on a throttle that meters engine air. Closed loop offers feed forward, clamps, deadband and an integral clamp.
  Its error accumulator rises while position error is > 6 % and decays otherwise (at a rate set by Fault Delay). Reaching 100 % in mode On raises faults 124-125 and disables the bridge until a power cycle, loss of the enable input, a stall (if configured) or a mode change.
  Setup mode tracks the accumulator without tripping: tune there and log the accumulator to separate a bad open/closed calibration from poor PID. In open loop, position reads 0 or 100 % while holding and 50 % while moving. Running means above 400 rpm, until 0 rpm.
- `vendor-doc` The shift light has per-gear points and needs gear detection; with no gear detected, the gear 0 points are used (see `g4x-g5-control.md`, Disputed). The example uses 10 Hz PWM with a duty ramp of 10 → 90 %.
- `vendor-doc` The engine fan's driven-speed lockout (5 km/h hysteresis; 0 = off) stops the fan above a road speed where ram air should be enough; if it is set too low, the fan stops before the airflow can cool the radiator. If ECT is above the fan threshold at power-on, a run-on timer runs the fan with the engine off. CAN-commanded fans (350Z and Patrol Y61 modes) still need an assigned output or a spare virtual aux, otherwise the fan status is never worked out.
- `vendor-doc` Alternator control (from G4X 6.22.8; G5) runs a PID on a PWM output (more duty means more charge) toward a voltage target table, with a feed-forward table and clamps. With the engine stopped it is off and the error is 0. It is unsuitable for internally regulated two-level alternators (S2000). The NB MX5 example takes the OEM frequency from a scope and uses P 32, I 0.4, D 0, about 35 % feed forward at no-load idle, and feed-forward axes of rpm and an electrical-load estimate.
- `vendor-doc` The tacho (6.19.37 on) has a max rpm cap (0 = none) and a needle offset, which are useful only once the multiplier is right. The speedo output is Hz = km/h × multiplier + offset, with a max speed cap and an optional key-on sweep. Set it up in order: calibrate the speed source, then set the multiplier for the rate, then the offset for a fixed error (+3 if the gauge reads 3 km/h low).
- `vendor-doc` Starter control cranks only from 0 rpm and needs Start Position active plus every lockout met (optional anti-theft, neutral or park, clutch, zero wheel speed). It releases above the deactivation rpm (550-600 rpm in the examples). In Start/Stop mode, a second press while running cuts fuel and spark down to 0 rpm, so a start input that re-triggers while running explains an odd engine kill in Start/Stop or Touch Start.
- `vendor-doc` The CAN anti-theft Default State is either immobilised until a CAN "off" request, or running until a CAN "on" request. It is a possible cause of a no-start when the fuel and spark setup is sound.
- `vendor-doc` Power architecture differs. A G4X is powered whenever +14 V is present, so +14 V must switch with the main relay, along with all aux-driven loads; the Aux 9/10 supply pins feed those high-side drivers. On a G5, +14 V is switched internally from the ignition switch pin (so +14 V may be permanent battery), and the e-throttle supply has an internal cut-off; Voodoo Pro adds an internal hold-power switch and relay. On a G5 Voodoo wire-in, the ignition/injector relay and the ECU main supply come from the same source.
- `vendor-doc` When swapping wire-ins on one A loom, note that a G4X starts on +14 V alone, while a G5 Voodoo Pro (and other G5s using that pin) needs the ignition switch pin live. A6 is an 8 V output on Storm, Xtreme and Fury, and the knock input on Atom and Monsoon. Fitting a G5 to an older loom means putting ignition-switched 12 V onto A6 and moving 8 V-fed sensors to 12 V or 5 V; hold power is not possible with that scheme.
### CAN, OBD and keypads
- `vendor-doc` There are two user CAN modules, with 11- and 29-bit IDs and 8-byte frames.
- `vendor-doc` User CAN scaling differs from G4+. Values are sent in displayed metric units (kPa, m, lambda, C, km/h) with the fraction truncated, so resolution needs a multiplier (75.3 kPa ×10 → 753; 12.75 V ×1 → 12), and the receiver divides back (503 / 10 → 50.3 % APS). G4+ help sends internal integers with fixed offsets (see Disputed).
  Status values are mostly 0 inactive, 1 active and 2 off; CAN DIs also use 3 for fault. GPS latitude and longitude carry an extra fixed 10^7 factor. Signed versus unsigned changes how received data is decoded.
- `vendor-doc` To find OEM scaling, tabulate raw bus numbers against known values. Cases in the help: ABS speeds in 0.01 km/h steps take divider 100, go into CAN DI Freq 1-4 and serve as wheel speed sources with calibration 0; a dash expecting rpm in 0.25 rpm steps takes multiplier 4; status bits take 1 and 1. Verify by driving and comparing the runtimes.
- `vendor-doc` The Generic Dash stream keeps G4+-style offsets: MGP is raw − 100 kPa; temperatures are raw − 50 C; ignition is 0.1 deg/count − 100 deg; lambda is 0.001/count; knock is 5/count; pulse width is 0.001 ms/count. Values are 16-bit unsigned, little endian, with byte 0 as the frame index. Dash2Pro is big endian, with four IDs at a fixed 20 Hz. Haltech IC7 emulation repeats the global knock value on both sensor channels.
- `vendor-doc` Only one CAN file can be loaded at once; it holds ≤ 15 channels, needs bus mode User Defined & CAN File, and is not stored in the tune or basemap. Save the setup from the ECU before loading a different file, or it is lost; a tune passed on without its CAN file will not recreate the CAN behaviour.
- `vendor-doc` Third-party IDs: CANchecked apps give IDs in hex (705 hex = 1797 decimal in PCLink); Haltech TCA-4/8 are supplied as ID set 716/717 or 730/731; the AEM X-Series UEGO uses extended ID 384 at gauge ID 1, +1 for each further gauge ID. Received EGTs go to CAN TC channels, which can be used as axes.
- `vendor-doc` Keypads are CANopen only (no J1939), with a maximum of two. A third-party keypad needs heartbeat off, state sent every 1500 ms, boot message off and active at power-up, otherwise its CAN DIs time out to Fault. Increment buttons wrap around, and a 1.5 s hold resets them. A keypad value ≥ 1 counts as active.
- `vendor-doc` With a Link Razor PDM (≤ 4), the ECU transmits CAN aux status, frequency and duty, and logs PDM output status, duty, current, ADIO and temperature. The base ID and the next 8 must be free; frequency inputs exist only on PDM ADIO 5-8.
- `vendor-doc` OBD-II over CAN works on either bus, with the module not set to Off (User Defined if it is only wired to the port). PID 04 is Load(Abs) clamped to 0-100 %; per-bank fuel trims come from closed-loop lambda; commanded equivalence ratio is the lambda target; PID 59 rail pressure is fuel pressure + baro; PID 52 is ethanol %.
- `vendor-doc` CAN vehicle modes put OEM data in fixed channels: wheel speeds normally in CAN Freq 1-4, buttons and requests in CAN DIs, and warning lamps in CAN Aux 1-4. A factory TCU gear and shifter need Gear Detection set to Off/CAN.
  Modes that transmit Estimated Torque (GT86, WRX 2015+, Mini R56, GM Global A TCU, Fiat/Alfa) require torque management to be set up, otherwise other modules receive garbage. Several modes are marked as lightly tested.
  Y61 Gen 2 does not receive the automatic's shift torque-reduction request. Polaris RZR uses the Speed Limit value even with that function off. On Altezza the body bus is BEAN, through an internal converter on CAN 2 at 1 Mbit/s; leave CAN 2 on its supplied streams and put add-ons on CAN 1.
- `vendor-doc` GM Global A automatics (G4X 6.31.x / G5 7.8.x or later; tested on a 6L) keep the factory TCU. The ECU sends engine speed, wheel speeds, pedal, throttle, temperatures, baro and Estimated Torque, and receives shaft speed, gear, shifter and the min and max torque requests. Upshift cut and downshift blip require torque management with Overall Torque Request on the e-throttle target axis; shift points require a TCU reflash. Versions 6.30.2/7.7.2 read the min torque request through CAN DI/analog channels instead.
- `vendor-doc` If TTLink loses USB once a CAN device shares the CAN/serial connector, the cause is noise on unused serial wires in the cable; use a cable without them.
### Logging
- `vendor-doc` (G5 record) ECU logging allows ≤ 250 parameters, each ≤ 1 kHz, with a total ≤ 100,000 samples/s (4 bytes each). It runs only while all enabled conditions are true, plus an off-delay, or until a dedicated off input has been on for the off-delay.
  Logging pauses while the trigger scope or the log download window is open, so a gap during scoping is expected. This caps the training's "about 1000 Hz" as a per-channel limit; the records give no G4X-specific limit. G4X Atom and pre-V3.0 Monsoon offer a digital trigger scope only.
- `vendor-doc` PC logging runs online and keeps a rolling buffer at the full link rate, which can be prepended to a new log and also sets the snake-trail length. The requested PC rate is limited by the link, so knock, misfire and trigger errors belong in ECU logging. Loading a PC logging set into ECU logging sets every channel to 50 Hz, so recheck the rates. Automatic PC logging can trigger on rpm, MAP or throttle.
- `vendor-doc` A histogram of MGP coloured by lambda over a selected time span shows the dwell in each load band and the mixture there; sparse bands give unreliable averages.

## Disputed / conflicts

- **Lambda channels fed only by CAN.** Help topics differ: Wideband Lambda Sensors gives Lambda 3-8; the Lambda Avg runtime gives 1-2 as analog or CAN and 3-12 as CAN only, with up to 12 averaged; CAN-Lambda gives a maximum of eight modules. The runtime page probably has the newer count; confirm on the unit.
- **Wheel speed naming across generations.** G4+ (`semantics-control.md`) phrases slip as "driven over undriven", with driven meaning powered. In the G4X/G5 help (Slip Calculation; Chassis and Body runtimes), powered wheels are Driving and unpowered wheels are Driven, with slip = (Driving − Driven) / Driven. The physics is the same but the sense of "driven" is opposite, so never read the G4+ wording as a G4X channel name. Some help records seem to use both labels for the powered wheels (fan lockout and fuel per distance, compared with CAN vehicle modes); follow the explicit Slip Calculation definition.
- **Logging rate.** Training gives about 1000 Hz on G4X. The ECU Logging topic, 7.8.2 (a record scoped to G5), gives a per-parameter max of 1 kHz, 250 parameters and 100,000 samples/s in total. The two agree if the training figure is read as a per-channel ceiling, not a whole-log rate; the records give no G4X figure.
- **User CAN scaling.** G4+ 5.6.8 help: internal integer plus fixed offsets (temperatures −50, ignition −100); status 1 active, 0 off. G4X/G5 7.8.2: displayed metric value, truncated, and scaled by a multiplier or divider; statuses 0 inactive, 1 active, 2 off. Generic Dash keeps the offset style. Copying the numbers does not port a G4+ CAN setup.
- **Dash rpm multiplier.** A G4+ help example using multiplier 4 for rpm to a dash has been judged wrong where the dash counts once per 4 rpm. The G4X/G5 example says that dash wants rpm in 0.25 rpm units, for which 4 is correct. The two may concern different dashes; re-examine the units in the G4+ example before labelling it an error.
- **Link 3 bar MAP calibration.** The Sensor Calibrations topic (7.8.2) gives a linear curve from about 0.3 V @ 20 kPa to 4.9 V @ 300 kPa. Release notes G4X 6.24.370 / G5 7.1.370 corrected the 4.75 V point from 290 → 300 kPa. Both put about 300 kPa at the top, but at different voltages; check the table in the PCLink version in use.
